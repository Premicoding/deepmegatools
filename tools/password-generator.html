Why Do You Need a Random Password Generator?

Most people make the critical mistake of using easily guessable information for their passwords, such as pet names, birthdays, or common words like "password123". Hackers use automated software that can crack these weak passwords in a matter of seconds. A Random Password Generator creates complex, unpredictable strings of characters that are virtually impossible to guess.

💡 Did you know? Over 80% of data breaches are caused by weak or reused passwords. A password with 16 random characters takes centuries for a supercomputer to crack!

5 Golden Rules for a Hack-Proof Password

  1. Length is King: Always aim for at least 12 to 16 characters.
  2. Mix It Up: Use a combination of letters (upper and lower), numbers, and symbols.
  3. No Personal Info: Never use names, phone numbers, or dates of birth.
  4. Never Reuse: Use a different password for every single account.
  5. Use 2FA: Enable Two-Factor Authentication wherever possible.

What is the difference between a Password and a Passphrase?

A password is usually a shorter string of random characters (e.g., x7#kL9@q), while a passphrase is a sequence of words (e.g., tiger-sunset-guitar). Passphrases are often easier to remember but still provide high security due to their length.

What does "Exclude Similar Characters" mean?

Similar characters include letters and numbers that look alike, such as 'O' (capital o), '0' (zero), 'I' (capital i), 'l' (lowercase L), and '1' (one). Excluding them prevents confusion when you read or type your password manually.

How often should I change my passwords?

You should change a password immediately if you suspect a data breach. Otherwise, using a highly strong, unique password combined with Two-Factor Authentication is generally sufficient without needing frequent changes.
